      Operations

      Each operation turns the day-count Y between two X-Dates into a day-offset Z, added to the earlier anchor (X1+) or the later one (X2+). An alpha operation's hit is worth 1 point, a beta's ½.

      Numbers, Y, the constants, + − * / % ^ and brackets. A lower-case x means multiply. Functions: oph_round, oph_flip (reverse the digits), oph_sqrt, oph_abs, oph_floor, oph_ceil, oph_log, oph_exp, oph_sin, oph_cos, oph_tan.

        What the numbers mean

        Guide

        The short version. Every button is explained in MANUAL.md, and README.md gets you going in two minutes; both are in the natorion folder.

        The loop

        1. X-Dates. Two or more anchor dates, oldest first.
        2. Y. For every pair of enabled X-Dates, Y is the number of days between them — whole days in Days scope, sunsets in HH:MM scope.
        3. Operations. Each turns Y into Z days, counted from the earlier (X1) or the later (X2) date of the pair.
        4. Z-Dates. Every projection landing on the same day merges into one Z-Date. It scores for each hit and for each MSRF match of its Z-value.
        5. Filters and sort. By default only Z-Dates after the last X-Date and after today are shown, up to 2559 days out.

        Scoring

        Each operation hit adds the operation's weight: 1 for alpha, ½ for beta. A Z-value that matches an MSRF number adds 1 (Normal) or 2 (Important, Vortex). Under the current system (v8 and later), the best MSRF class multiplies instead: ×1.5 for Normal, ×2 for Important or Vortex, and its first match is left out of the points it multiplies.

        score = (operation points + MSRF points, less the multiplying match) × best multiplier

        A Z-value matches a Vortex number within 0.1. Otherwise it is rounded to a whole number and looked up — except that a value ending in exactly .5 matches nothing, because it leans to neither side.

        The three MSRF sets

        Scope

        Days counts calendar days in UTC, as v12 did. HH:MM · sunset reads each X-Date as a local time at the event's latitude and longitude, counts days as sunsets, and reports each Z-Date as the window from one sunset to the next.

        Resonance marks

        The Marks column adds the Chronicon's readings to each Z-Date: ⮌ when its MMDDYYYY form is a palindrome, 19 or 138 when its distance in days from today is a multiple of that number, and a glyph when a new moon (🌑), full moon (🌕) or eclipse falls within a day of it. The other moon phases can be switched on as timeline layers.

        How this was rebuilt

        Ophis v12 is an offline Electron app whose source ships un-obfuscated. Natorion re-derives its engine from that source and runs in any browser with no install and no server. tests/parity.js runs the original v12 code and this engine side by side on the sample files and on randomly generated events, and checks that every Z-Date, score, hit count and match agrees.

        Deliberate differences:

        • Equations are parsed by a small grammar, never compiled with new Function(), which closes v12's code-execution hole in shared .oph files. ^ now means power; v12 validated it as power and then ran it as XOR.
        • Nothing a file supplies is ever written into the page as HTML.
        • Moon phases are exact instants from Astronomy Engine, not a once-a-day approximation.
        • At high latitudes near the equinoxes, v12 swapped its sunset library for less accurate ones; Natorion keeps the accurate one.
        • In HH:MM scope, T-Dates are read at the event's location (v12 read them in the computer's own time zone).
        • An operation whose result is not a number for some Y is skipped for that Y instead of failing the whole run.
        • Time zones come from your browser's current time-zone database. Ophis v12 shipped 2023 data, so places whose rules changed since (Kazakhstan moved to UTC+5 in 2024, for one) show local times an hour apart in HH:MM scope. The instants and scores are the same.

        Ophis is a study instrument after the work of Jason Breshears (Archaix). Natorion studies and rebuilds the software; it makes no claim that the projections predict anything.
