Natorion CipherNotes

Two tools beside the v12 engine. They read its numbers and never change its scores: the MSRF Magnitude (how many dimensions of arithmetic a number appears in) and the Phi Shadow.

MSRF Magnitude

Type a Z-value or MSRF number. The app class comes from v12's own match rule; the tier and dimension count come from the v2.0 hierarchy.

Phi Shadow

Divide by φ. The difference between the answer and the root is the shadow, and it equals the answer divided by φ again: x − x/φ = x/φ².

Shadow ladder

The root divided by φ again and again. Each rung is the shadow of the rung two above it, checked against the MSRF table.

360 → 222.49 → 137.51: the shadow of a full circle is the golden angle, 137.5°, whose nearest whole is 138 (Tier II). v12 itself does not count 137.5 as a match: a value ending in exactly .5 is never one.

Use it as an Operation

The shadow written in the Operations grammar. Click one to copy it, then paste it into the try-it box or add it as an extra operation.

With OPH_PHI = 1.618 the two forms differ in the third decimal (for Y = 138: 52.714 against 52.710). With the true φ they are the same number.

The tiers

A hierarchy by orders of magnitude. Click a number to load it into the calculator. Apex Projections (Tier VII) that target a date also hit by a lower tier override the Core Algorithm's dates.

The datasets, filters and tier work are Jason Breshears' (Archaix), from Jason’s original website and the v2.0 data dump. The magnitude reading and the tier compilation follow the community paper Ophis MSRF Details: Sources for the MSRF Magnitude Calculator. Data notes: 468 is listed twice in Tier I and 480 sits in Tiers I and III (counted in III); 63 numbers in the v12 table have no tier yet. This is a pattern tool for study, not a betting tool.
